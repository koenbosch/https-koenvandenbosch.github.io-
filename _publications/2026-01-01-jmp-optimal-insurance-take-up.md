Implicit insurance subsidies in housing markets protect households from growing weather damages but distort private insurance markets, undermining the long-run availability of insurance. I model a housing market with insurance intermediaries in a dynamic general equilibrium setting. High insurance take-up mitigates housing fire sales following a weather shock but generates insurance market crashes whose steepness is governed by the economy-wide marginal propensity to insure. A social planner internalizes the pecuniary externality of possible insurance market crashes, and the socially optimal insurance take-up is lower than in the unregulated equilibrium. The time-consistent social planner's actions can be decentralized with a subsidy or tax on insurance that aims to jointly mitigate housing fire sales and the frequency and magnitude of insurance market collapses. I further provide empirical evidence consistent with the amplifying role of insurer financing constraints in insurance market withdrawals.

Presented at: ERIM PhD Seminar.
