#### [A Theory of Optimal Disaster Insurance under Intermediary Constraints]({{ site.url }}/files/jmp.pdf) <span class="paper-tag jmp">Job Market Paper</span>
{: .paper-title}

**Talks:** ERIM PhD Seminar
{: .paper-meta}

<details class="paper-abstract-toggle">
<summary>Abstract</summary>
<div class="paper-abstract">
<p>Implicit insurance subsidies in housing markets protect households from growing weather damages but distort private insurance markets, undermining the long-run availability of insurance. I model a housing market with insurance intermediaries in a dynamic general equilibrium setting. High insurance take-up mitigates housing fire sales following a weather shock but generates insurance market crashes whose steepness is governed by the economy-wide marginal propensity to insure. A social planner internalizes the pecuniary externality of possible insurance market crashes, and the socially optimal insurance take-up is lower than in the unregulated equilibrium. The time-consistent social planner's actions can be decentralized with a subsidy or tax on insurance that aims to jointly mitigate housing fire sales and the frequency and magnitude of insurance market collapses. I further provide empirical evidence consistent with the amplifying role of insurer financing constraints in insurance market withdrawals.</p>
</div>
</details>

#### Financial Stability and Natural Disasters under Endogenous Adaptation
{: .paper-title}

*with Christina Kolerus and Joana Pereira*
{: .paper-coauthors}

**Talks:** Banque de France, Stockholm School of Economics, IMF
{: .paper-meta}

<details class="paper-abstract-toggle">
<summary>Abstract</summary>
<div class="paper-abstract">
<p>We examine the effect of natural disasters (ND) on financial stability in insurance and mortgage markets when household adaptation is endogenous to changing financial conditions. We first empirically establish that ND reduce insurers' and banks' solvency and profitability metrics, although the effects for banks are small. Using a novel instrument based on reinsurance market shocks, we show that the sensitivity of bank financials to ND rises with the size of the insurance gap, revealing interaction effects. Using a structural model with heterogeneous households, we then analyze how the responses of banks and insurers following ND dynamically shape moral hazard and credit constraints, affecting investment in physical resilience against ND and therefore long-run financial stability. Consistent with the empirical results, disaster-driven default rates increase when insurance gaps grow. Yet, endogenous household adaptation substantially lowers the long-run financial consequences of ND relative to the counterfactual of no adaptation. We further illustrate the implications of moral hazard and credit constraints for the optimal mix between insurance- and adaptation subsidies when the aim is to reduce defaults.</p>
</div>
</details>

#### [The Informational Role of Sustainable Investors](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5183758)
{: .paper-title}

*with Marta Szymanowska*
{: .paper-coauthors}

**Talks:** Cunef University Madrid*, FMA Annual Meeting, French Finance Association Conference, Erasmus DoIP Seminar, Tri-City Bridge Workshop, ERIM PhD Seminar &nbsp;<small>(*presented by co-author)</small>
{: .paper-meta}

**&#9733; Award:** Semifinalist, FMA Annual Meeting Best Paper Award (2025)
{: .paper-award}

<details class="paper-abstract-toggle">
<summary>Abstract</summary>
<div class="paper-abstract">
<p>Recent evidence suggests that sustainable investors may weaken information transmission into asset prices while being simultaneously classified as active traders. Using a demand-based asset pricing approach, we examine the behavior of sustainable investors and their impact on price informativeness. We find that sustainable investors are price elastic, enhancing informativeness by trading on both sustainable characteristics and long-term fundamentals. Stocks with strong sustainable demand exhibit greater price elasticity and responsiveness to idiosyncratic news, though this effect is weaker for green stocks, where sustainable investors trade less actively. Importantly, identifying sustainable investors outside a demand system misattributes passive demand as sustainable, distorting the estimated impact of sustainable investors. Overall, sustainable investors play a dual role: acting as active traders across their portfolios while behaving as preference-driven holders of the sustainable stocks.</p>
</div>
</details>
